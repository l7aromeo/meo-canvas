# Code of Conduct

## The standard

Everyone taking part in this project — issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions — is expected to
behave decently. Concretely:

- Criticise the code, not the person. Reviews here can be blunt about a change; they are not blunt
  about whoever wrote it.
- Assume the other person is trying. Most bad patches are inexperience, not carelessness, and most
  vague bug reports come from someone who does not yet know which detail mattered.
- Accept that "no" is an answer. A maintainer declining a feature is not an invitation to relitigate
  it across three threads.
- Leave people's identity out of it. Harassment, slurs, and unwelcome personal attention are not
  disagreements about software, and they are not tolerated at any level of technical contribution.
- Do not publish other people's private information.

## Scope

This applies in every project space — the repository, its issues and pull requests, its releases —
and to conduct elsewhere that is directed at someone because of their participation here.

## Reporting

Report to <ukasyahrz@outlook.com>. Reports stay between you and the maintainer; whoever is reported
does not learn who reported them, and a report is not answered by forwarding it to them.

Say what happened and where. Links help. You do not need to have a resolution in mind.

## Enforcement

The maintainer decides what happens, in proportion to what occurred and whether it is a pattern:

1. A private word, saying what was wrong and why.
2. A public correction, where the behaviour was public.
3. A temporary block from the project's spaces.
4. A permanent one.

Serious incidents can start at any step. A maintainer involved in an incident does not rule on it.
