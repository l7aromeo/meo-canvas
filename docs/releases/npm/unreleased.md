### Fixed

- A line whose leading is odd or not a whole pixel puts its baseline where Chrome does, half the leading floored, for a box taller or shorter than its text alike: 12px Oswald at `lineHeight: 1` drew its baseline 11.5px below the line's top where Chrome draws it at 11, and 13px at `lineHeight: 1.5` at 15.75 where Chrome draws it at 15. Text on such lines drew up to three quarters of a pixel low, and `alignItems: 'baseline'` aligned to that baseline. Boxes are unchanged (`line-height-normal.tsv`, `l7aromeo/meo-canvas#233`).
- A line at `line-height: normal`, a text node with no `lineHeight`, includes its font's line gap, as Chrome does. From 11.0.0 it was left out, so on a font with a gap every line was shorter than Chrome's and its baseline sat higher: 16px Poppins, whose gap is a tenth of its em, made a 23px line with its baseline at 17 where Chrome makes 25 and 18, and two lines were 46px against 50. A font with no gap is unchanged. Each face on a line adds its own gap, split between above its ascent and below its descent with any odd pixel below, and the line box takes the largest extent on each side, so 25px Oswald beside 25px Poppins is 41px with its baseline at 30, as in Chrome (`line-height-normal.tsv`, `l7aromeo/meo-canvas#232`).
