### Added

- `frame` takes a negative index, counting from the end: `frame: -1` draws the last frame of an animated image and `-2` the one before it. A source with one frame ignores a negative index as it does a positive one.

- `justifyItems` and `justifySelf` place a grid item or a block child along the inline axis with every value CSS gives them, spelled as CSS spells them: `'normal'`, `'stretch'`, `'baseline'`, `'first baseline'`, `'last baseline'`, the positions `'center'`, `'start'`, `'end'`, `'self-start'`, `'self-end'`, `'flex-start'`, `'flex-end'`, `'left'` and `'right'`, each also after `'safe '` or `'unsafe '`, `'auto'` on `justifySelf`, and `'legacy'` and `'legacy left'`, `'legacy right'` and `'legacy center'`, in either order (`'left legacy'`, `'right legacy'`, `'center legacy'`), on `justifyItems`. Absent is CSS's `auto` and initial `legacy`, which takes an ancestor's `legacy` position through nodes stating nothing. A grid column's items asking for `'baseline'` or `'last baseline'` align their left border edges at its start or end, as Chrome does, and a flex item ignores both properties. `'anchor-center'`, alone or after `'safe '` or `'unsafe '`, on `justifySelf` is refused with a `TypeError` saying a scene has no anchor positioning, and any other spelling on either property with one naming the property and what it takes (`l7aromeo/meo-canvas#171`).

### Changed

- A chart given a negative value throws `[canvas] a chart cannot draw a negative value (got N)`; the message no longer compares the refusal with the previous major version.
- The page root's background colour fills the whole canvas beneath every effect the root carries, as the root element's does in Chrome: a root with `opacity` below 1, a `mask` or a `borderRadius` draws its colour opaque and out to the canvas's corners, with its content, gradient and image over it. Its `filter` applies to the whole canvas; its border and shadows stay on its box.
- A `frame` past either end of an animated image is refused with `node N asks for frame F of an image with M frames`, naming the index as written and the frame count, rather than as an image whose bytes are in no format this decodes.
- An image whose source cannot be fetched or decoded takes the box Chrome gives a broken `<img>` with no `alt`, rather than collapsing on every axis it does not size. With only a `width` it was that wide and 0 tall, so its placeholder drew nothing; an `auto` axis nothing stretches is 16 instead, so `Image({ src: { url }, width: 100 })` is 100x16, and as a block child or a stretched grid or flex item it fills its container (300x16 in a 300-wide block, the whole cell in a grid) as Chrome's does. It is laid out in that box under every `onImageError`, and an image with both sizes stated is unchanged (`l7aromeo/meo-canvas#183`).

### Fixed

- A text item that wraps is as wide as the space it is offered, as in Chrome, rather than as wide as its widest line, wherever it is not stretched: in a flex column under `alignItems: 'flex-start'` or `'center'`, and in a container sized to its content, such as an absolutely positioned box with only a `maxWidth`. In a 200-wide column with 50 of horizontal margin it was 137 wide where Chrome gives 150. Right- and centre-aligned lines move with the box, and an item cut to one line by `maxLines` is the full 150 rather than 134. Stretched items, rows and blocks were already right (`l7aromeo/meo-canvas#162`).
- An image URL whose host does not resolve is reported in `canvas.warnings` as `failure: 'host-not-found'` (do not retry) rather than `'transport'` (retry). A lookup that could not finish, because the resolver was unreachable or said to try again, stays `'transport'`.
- An SVG image given `frame: 1` or later draws, as a still raster does, rather than being refused.
- The page root's `margin` and `position` place it as Chrome places `<html>`, rather than being ignored: an unsized root fills the canvas less its margins, a percentage resolving against the canvas width on either axis, `'auto'` counting as no margin and a negative margin growing it; under `positionType: 'relative'` a `position` offset moves it without resizing it, while a root left `'static'`, the default, ignores it; and a page as tall as its content counts the root's vertical margins. Its background still covers the whole canvas, its gradient and image tiling from the root's box. A stated `width` and `height` are still the canvas size.
- An `Image` grid item under the default alignment draws at its intrinsic size, as Chrome does, rather than stretched to fill its cell. `alignItems: 'stretch'` still stretches it, its width following its ratio.
- A `transform`'s `translateX` and `translateY` are applied after its `rotate` and `scale`, as CSS applies `translate` after `rotate` and `scale`, rather than being rotated and scaled with the box. A box translated 20px and scaled 2 moves 20px, not 40px, and a 20px box at the page's top-left corner, translated 20px and turned 90° about that corner, covers x 0 to 19 rather than leaving the canvas. A transform with only a translation, or none, draws as before (`l7aromeo/meo-canvas#166`).
- A `frame` that is not a whole number, or a `frame` or `zIndex` outside -2147483648 to 2147483647, is refused naming the property (`frame is 1.5; it takes a whole number`) rather than a slot the caller never wrote (`slot 41 holds 1.5, which is not an integer`).
- A box that states no `direction` takes its parent's, as it does in Chrome: under a `direction: 'rtl'` parent a grid runs its tracks from the right, a flex row starts at the right, a block's child under a box stating no direction sits at the right, and `textAlign: 'start'` and `'end'` align to the right and left edges. An explicit `direction` still overrides the inherited one (`l7aromeo/meo-canvas#165`).
- A wrapped text box draws the lines its layout measured. Layout lays text out at a fractional width and rounds the box to whole pixels, and the text was then wrapped again at the rounded width, so a paragraph given `width: 137.2` with a line just over 137 wide drew a third line below its two-line, 48-tall box; it draws two, as Chrome does. A box less than a pixel narrower than its text, such as a label 82.14 wide at `width: 82`, breaks it, as Chrome does, rather than drawing one line wider than the box.
- An `Image` draws its picture where Chrome draws it when the box or the fit falls between pixels: the picture is fitted in the box as laid out, before it was rounded, and each of its edges is snapped to a whole pixel, a half rounding up. A namecard picture of 840x400 in a box 350 wide and 166.67 tall was scaled under `objectFit: 'cover'` from the 167-tall rounded box, 350.7 wide and a third of a pixel to the left, so it differed from `'fill'` across the whole box by up to 29 per channel; the two are the same picture, as in Chrome. `'contain'`, `'none'` and `'scale-down'` that leave an odd free space, such as `'contain'` at 16.5 in a 333x150 box, draw from the next whole pixel rather than across two. An SVG source under `'fill'` in a box of another aspect lays its drawing out under its own `meet` inside the box, and that drawing is snapped in the same way. (`l7aromeo/meo-canvas#185`).
- A `Path` with a `viewBox` is fitted into its content box, inside its padding and border, as Chrome maps an `<svg>`'s `viewBox`, and at the box's size before rounding. With 7px of padding and a 3px border it drew across the whole box, 8px outside Chrome's drawing at the sides and the top; in a box 166.67 tall it was scaled as though 167, which moved a point ten times the `viewBox`'s height down by 3.4px (`l7aromeo/meo-canvas#185`).
- An `Image` in block flow keeps a declared `height`, and a `maxHeight`, below the height its picture's aspect ratio would give its width, as Chrome does: one 200 wide over 60x40 art with `height: 60` or `maxHeight: 60` was laid out 133 tall, the ratio's height, where Chrome gives 60. A `minHeight` above that maximum still wins over it. A flex container was already right, and so was a declared height above the ratio's. The same held for any node without children that has an `aspectRatio`, in block flow (`l7aromeo/meo-canvas#189`).
- A background image lands where Chrome puts it. Along an axis it does not repeat on, its position and its size are each rounded to a whole pixel, a half up, where they were truncated and left fractional: a 300x100 box with `size: { width: '50%', height: '25%' }` centred drew its picture from y 37 rather than 38, and a 350x167 one at 62 to 103.72 rather than 63 to 105. Along an axis it repeats on, its phase is no longer truncated: a 33.3-wide tile centred in 300 starts a third of a pixel later, as in Chrome. A tile the box cuts off draws its visible part at the rounded size (`l7aromeo/meo-canvas#186`).
- The API reference described `lossless` as encoding WebP without loss, and every WebP encode given it throws ``encoding to WebP failed: invalid `lossless`: webp is either lossless already or has no lossless form``. `lossless` is AVIF's: meo-skia-canvas reads it for AVIF alone and refuses it elsewhere. The reference says so, and says that a lossless WebP is `quality: 1`, as meo-skia-canvas documents (`l7aromeo/meo-canvas#184`).
- An `Image` with a declared `width` keeps it under a `minHeight` whose height its picture's aspect ratio would turn into a wider width, as Chrome does: one 200 wide over 60x40 art with `minHeight: 160` was laid out 240x160, the ratio's width at that height, where Chrome gives 200x160. It held in block flow, as a flex item, in a flex column and out of flow, with a length or a percentage width, as a grid item with a percentage width, and for any node with an `aspectRatio`. An `Image` with no `width` still takes its width from the minimum, 240x160, and a `minWidth` still wins over the declared width (`l7aromeo/meo-canvas#192`).
- An `Image` with a declared `width` keeps it under a `maxHeight` below the height its picture's aspect ratio gives that width, as Chrome does, rather than being narrowed to the ratio's width at the maximum: one 200 wide over 60x40 art with `maxHeight: 60` was laid out 90x60 as a flex item, where Chrome gives 200x60. It held as a flex item stretched or not, in a flex column, as a grid item and out of flow, for a percentage width too, and for any node with an `aspectRatio`, in block flow as well. A growing flex item grows past its declared width, a `maxWidth` still wins, and an `Image` with no `width` still takes its width from the maximum (`l7aromeo/meo-canvas#193`).
- A gradient stop that is not opaque mixes as Chrome mixes it. A `gradient` on a node, the root's included, and a gradient `mask` mix with the alpha multiplied in, as CSS specifies: red fading to `'transparent'` or to `'rgba(255, 0, 0, 0)'` drew a grey band, rgb(191, 129, 129) at the midpoint over white, where Chrome gives rgb(255, 129, 129), and red to `'rgba(0, 0, 255, 0.2)'` drew rgb(178, 103, 180) there for Chrome's rgb(229, 103, 128). A `Path`'s gradient `fill` or `stroke` mixes straight, as Chrome mixes an SVG gradient, so it is unchanged except that a stop at alpha 0 keeps its own colour: red to `'rgba(255, 0, 0, 0)'` no longer darkens. Every page `colorType` that keeps colour drew it the same way (`l7aromeo/meo-canvas#202`).
