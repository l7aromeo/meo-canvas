### Fixed

- A flex item stretched across its line did not take its main size from its
  aspect ratio. In a column with a height of its own, an item with
  `flex_grow(1.0)` and `aspect_ratio(1.0)` came out as wide as the line and
  only as tall as the line let it be — `424x248` in a `424x248` content box,
  where a browser makes it `424x424`.

  **The size CSS asks for here overflows its own line**, and that is the
  behaviour rather than a side effect of the repair. A stretched cross size is
  _definite_ (CSS Flexbox 1 §9.8), a definite cross size on an item with a
  ratio becomes the _transferred size suggestion_ (§4.5), and that is the
  item's **minimum** on the main axis — a minimum, so the line cannot clamp it
  back down. Measured against WebKit 26.6, Chromium 153 and Firefox 155: all
  three give `424x424`.

  **Your renders can move, and this is how to keep the old ones.**
  `.min_height(px(0.0))` on the item. It is not a workaround — the floor is the
  item's _automatic_ minimum, so naming any definite minimum replaces it, and
  this is the spelling CSS provides for the purpose. `.height(pct(100.0))`
  reaches the same result for a different reason, since a definite main size
  leaves nothing to derive, and so does `.overflow(Overflow::Hidden)`, since a
  scroll container has no automatic minimum at all. All three escapes hold the
  old `424x248`, and hold it in every engine.

  **A maximum is the wrong escape, and not because the answer is unsettled.**
  `max_height` as a percentage and as a length both give `424x248` here, which
  is what Chromium and Firefox give and what the specification's own wording
  gives — the minimum is "clamped by the maximum main size if it's definite",
  which bounds the height and says nothing about the width. **WebKit is the one
  that differs**, taking the clamped height back through the ratio to
  `248x248`. So a render written that way holds here, in Chrome and in Firefox,
  and moves in Safari; `.min_height(px(0.0))` holds in all of them, which is
  why it is the one to reach for. Both spellings are compared against Chromium
  like every other row rather than exempted, in
  `crates/meo-canvas/tests/assets/chrome/ratio-stretch-main.tsv`, so this is a
  behaviour under test rather than a claim in a note.

  **And these renders moved too.** An item already carrying a maximum came out
  `248x248` in `0.1.1` and comes out `424x248` here. The clamp holds the height
  where it always did; the width it used to shrink to was this same defect seen
  from the other side.

  **A second family moved, and it moves the other way.** Where the shape above
  gains height, these had their **cross** size derived from the main one rather
  than set by the stretch, so they come out narrower: `.aspect_ratio(2.0)` was
  `496x248` and is `424x248`, and an item whose content is taller than the
  ratio's answer was `600x600` and is `424x600`. The `max_height` rows above
  are the third member of it.

  **Twelve rows across the conformance tables move between `0.1.1` and
  `0.1.2`**, and the rest are the first shape under other spellings:
  `.flex_grow(0.0)`, padding, `BoxSizing::BorderBox`, two stretched items
  sharing a line — `424x124` to `424x424` — and `.aspect_ratio(0.5)`, which
  goes `424x248` to `424x848`.

  **It is a workaround rather than a fix, and it is marked as one in the
  source.** taffy does not give a stretched item the automatic minimum its
  ratio owes it; `[WORKAROUND]` in `crates/meo-canvas-core/src/layout.rs`
  carries the derivation, and the `align-items: stretch` row of
  `a_grown_main_size_never_reaches_the_ratio` in
  `crates/meo-canvas-core/tests/taffy_flex_ratio.rs` fails the day taffy stops
  needing it — so this behaviour can move again, toward the browser.
  `DioxusLabs/taffy#351` is the unimplemented section and
  `DioxusLabs/taffy#1182` proposes to close it; the issue this repository filed
  stays open until one of them lands, by design rather than by oversight.
